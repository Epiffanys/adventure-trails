Plan Before You Go

A good hike starts before you even get to the trail. Check the weather, distance, elevation, parking, and any permits you might need.

Pick a trail that fits your day

Easy trails are great for short walks, families, or beginners. Moderate trails are a bit longer or steeper. Hard trails usually take more time, effort, and planning.

Trail conditions can change quickly. Weather, closures, or seasonal changes can all affect your hike. Always check the official trail page before you go.

Bring: water, snacks, sun protection, layers, a map, and a charged phone.
Check: distance, elevation, parking, closures, permits, and trail surface.
Respect: wildlife, posted signs, other hikers, and Leave No Trace principles.

Easy Trails

Lower Yosemite Fall Trail

Yosemite National Park, California

This short loop leads to the base of Lower Yosemite Fall, one of the most popular waterfall views in Yosemite Valley. The trail is paved, mostly flat, and easy to follow. It is a good choice for beginners, families, or anyone who wants a quick scenic walk with a big payoff.

Pa'rus Trail

Zion National Park, Utah

The Pa'rus Trail is a paved path that follows the Virgin River through Zion Canyon. It has wide canyon views, gentle grades, and several places to stop and enjoy the scenery. This trail is a relaxed option for visitors who want an easy walk without taking on Zion’s steeper routes.

Congress Trail

Sequoia National Park, California

Congress Trail begins near the General Sherman Tree and takes hikers through a grove of giant sequoias. The path is paved and easy to follow, but it still feels peaceful once you move away from the busiest viewing areas. It is a great trail for seeing some of the park’s most famous trees up close.

Cook's Meadow Loop

Yosemite National Park, California

Cook's Meadow Loop is a short, flat walk in Yosemite Valley with views of Yosemite Falls, Half Dome, Glacier Point, and Sentinel Rock. It is a good trail when you want beautiful views without a long climb. The loop also works well as a calm introduction to the valley before exploring longer hikes.

Laurel Falls Trail

Great Smoky Mountains National Park, Tennessee

Laurel Falls Trail is a popular paved route that leads to an 80-foot waterfall. The trail has some uphill sections, but it is still one of the easier waterfall hikes in the park. Because it is well known and close to park roads, it can get crowded, so an early start is helpful.

Rim Trail

Grand Canyon National Park, Arizona

The Rim Trail follows the South Rim and offers many canyon viewpoints. Some sections are paved and mostly flat, so visitors can choose a short walk or a longer outing. It is a good option for seeing the Grand Canyon without hiking below the rim.

Moderate Trails

Emerald Lake Trail

Rocky Mountain National Park, Colorado

Emerald Lake Trail starts near Bear Lake and passes Nymph Lake and Dream Lake before reaching Emerald Lake. The route is not very long, but the elevation and rocky sections make it feel more challenging than an easy walk. It is a popular choice for hikers who want alpine lake views in a shorter distance.

Delicate Arch Trail

Arches National Park, Utah

Delicate Arch Trail climbs across open slickrock to one of the most recognized arches in the United States. The route has little shade, so heat can make it feel harder than the mileage suggests. This is a good hike for visitors who want a classic desert view and are prepared with water and sun protection.

Cadillac North Ridge Trail

Acadia National Park, Maine

Cadillac North Ridge Trail leads toward the summit of Cadillac Mountain, one of the best-known viewpoints in Acadia. The trail has steady climbing, open rock sections, and wide views of the coastline. It is a good moderate route for hikers who want a summit experience without choosing one of the park’s hardest climbs.

Hard Trails

Angels Landing

Zion National Park, Utah

Angels Landing is one of Zion’s most famous hikes and is known for steep climbing and exposed sections. The final chain section requires a permit, and the route is not a good choice for anyone uncomfortable with heights. Hikers should plan ahead, carry water, and check current permit rules before visiting.

Half Dome Day Hike

Yosemite National Park, California

The Half Dome Day Hike is a long and very strenuous route with major elevation gain. When the cables are up, hikers need a permit to continue to the summit. This hike requires an early start, strong preparation, and careful attention to weather because storms can make the route dangerous.

Bright Angel Trail

Grand Canyon National Park, Arizona

Bright Angel Trail descends below the South Rim and is much harder on the return climb. Hikers can choose different turnaround points, but even shorter sections require planning because of heat, elevation change, and limited shade. This trail is best for visitors who understand that hiking back up takes more effort than going down.

Skyline Trail

Skyline Trail is a demanding loop near Paradise with views of Mount Rainier, glaciers, wildflowers, and open alpine terrain. The trail has significant elevation gain and can still have snow depending on the season. It is a rewarding hike, but visitors should check conditions and be ready for changing mountain weather.

Grinnell Glacier Trail is a long hike with mountain views, lakes, and a steady climb toward glacier scenery. The distance and elevation gain make it a serious day hike for most visitors. Wildlife, snow, and weather can affect the route, so checking park updates before starting is important.

Hiking Tips

These simple tips can help your hike go more smoothly.

Pack the essentials

Bring water, snacks, sunscreen, and a small first-aid kit. Even short hikes can feel longer if the weather changes.

Match the trail to your group

Pick a trail based on the slowest person in your group. Things like distance, hills, and weather can make a hike feel harder.

Start early on popular trails

Starting early helps with parking, crowds, and heat. For longer hikes, give yourself extra time.

Leave no trace

Stay on the trail, pack out your trash, and leave everything as you found it.

Trail safety reminder

Conditions can change depending on the season. Check the trail page and weather before you go. If something feels off, it is okay to turn around.

Trail Notes

A few simple reminders for better hiking habits.

Know when to turn around

Getting to the end is not worth risking your safety. Turning around early is always the better choice.

Plan your food and water

Bring more water than you think you need, especially in hot areas. Snacks can also help keep your energy up.

Respect changing seasons

Weather and trail conditions can change quickly. Always check recent updates instead of relying on old information.
